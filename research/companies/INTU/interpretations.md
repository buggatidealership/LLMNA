# INTU — INTERPRETATIONS

**My inferred reads. Not facts.** Facts live in `facts.md`. Opened 2026-08-08.

## I1 — The de-rating is a duration verdict, and no quarterly print can falsify it

🟡 The most important thing to understand about Intuit at $325 is what the market is *not* saying. It is not saying FY27 will miss. Consensus, and even Goldman's Sell case, has earnings compounding high-single to low-double digits through FY28. The reverse-DCF says the price embeds **−2.5% cash-flow growth in perpetuity** after a decade of fade.

That is a **terminal-value verdict**: the claim that the price of performing a bounded knowledge-work task trends toward zero, and that Intuit's revenue *is* the price of performing bounded knowledge-work tasks.

The uncomfortable implication, stated because it is true: **a terminal-value claim cannot be falsified by two quarters of prints.** My falsifier set (F1–F4) tests the *near-term decay rate*, which is the best available instrument, but it is not the same instrument as the market's actual claim. Anyone buying this should know they are taking the other side of an argument their own falsifiers cannot fully adjudicate. That is a real limit on the position, not a rhetorical hedge.

## I2 — Two bear cases are being conflated, and only one has evidence

🟢 on the evidence, 🟡 on the read.

- **Bear A (the one the market articulates):** agentic AI displaces QuickBooks and TurboTax. An adversarial pass hunting specifically for this found **zero** customer-level evidence — no named competitor with traction, no counts, no accountant-channel defections, no trade-press coverage. This is narrative.
- **Bear B (the one that is actually true):** growth is price-carried. TurboTax units −2% with ARPU +11%; QBO list prices +41–70% on 2026-08-01 while QBO revenue growth decelerated 25→24→22.

**My read: the market has priced Bear A, and Bear B is what is actually happening.** These have very different terminal values. Bear A means the franchise dies. Bear B means it becomes a slower-growing, cash-generative, price-taking utility — which at 20.6× GAAP and an 8.4% FCF yield is a *fine* thing to own and a poor thing to price at 10× terminal.

The investable form of this thesis is therefore: **you get paid when the market re-prices from Bear A to Bear B.** Not when it becomes bullish.

## I3 — The Direct File result cuts both ways and I initially read it only one way

🟡 The government killed IRS Direct File on 2025-11-07, and TurboTax units *still* fell ~2% in the first season without it. The adversarial agent framed this as the strongest fact against the thesis, and as a control experiment it is genuinely damning for the volume story.

But the same fact contains a second reading: **Intuit's revenue grew 7% and TurboTax Live grew 36% in a year when its unit base shrank.** The franchise converted a shrinking DIY funnel into a growing assisted-service business. That is either (a) the last squeeze of a dying asset, or (b) a deliberate migration up the value chain into the part an LLM cannot serve. **I do not know which, and I am not going to pretend the data resolves it.** TurboTax Live passing 50% of TurboTax revenue with retention up 2 points is weak evidence for (b).

## I4 — Why Intuit rather than the cheaper de-rated software names

🟡 **This is a judgment, and the operator can reject it.** Adobe trades at roughly **10× forward earnings** on ~11.6% revenue growth — cheaper than Intuit on that lens. Salesforce ~13.8×. If the thesis were simply "de-rated incumbent software is mispriced," Adobe is the more extreme expression and I should have picked it.

My reason for preferring Intuit is a view about *which moat survives agents*, not about which multiple is lower:
- Adobe's core product is **the thing generative models do natively.** Image and video synthesis is the canonical model capability; the workflow is the moat and the workflow is attackable.
- Intuit's core product is a **system of record attached to regulated money movement.** The ledger is what the bank, the payroll provider, the tax authority, the lender and the accountant reconcile against. Payments and payroll are licensed, liability-bearing businesses. A model can do the *reasoning*; it cannot be the counterparty.

If that distinction is wrong, Adobe is the better buy and this file is mis-aimed. Recording it as a contestable judgment rather than burying it.

## I5 — The layoff tells us something, just not what the press said

🟢 on the quote, 🟡 on the read. Goodarzi: **"This was not about AI."** Several outlets reported the opposite. I had built a margin-tailwind model on the outlets' version and withdrew it.

My read on what it *does* mean: cutting 17% of staff while guiding revenue +13–14% and simultaneously announcing an **$8B buyback authorisation and a 15% dividend increase** is a company reallocating from headcount to capital returns. That is the behaviour of management that believes its cash flows are durable and its stock is cheap — **or** of management harvesting a franchise it expects to shrink. The two are observationally identical right now. **Sep 17 Investor Day is where they have to pick one in public.**

## I6 — Why this name and not the three that nearly beat it

🟡 The 2026-08-08 sweep surfaced three genuine rivals, all with the same *shape* — a name crushed by a narrative while its numbers held:

- **Nintendo (7974.T)** — the closest call, and arguably a cleaner *edge*: Q1 FY27 delivered **38.5% of full-year operating-profit guidance in the seasonally weakest quarter** and guidance was not raised. That is arithmetic, not a multiple opinion. I ranked it second because (a) revenue *fell* 9.5% — the profit surge is mix plus a ~¥45bn one-off tariff refund; (b) it is a hardware cycle in year two, and console cycles roll over; (c) TSE 100-share lots (~€4,600), higher DeGiro fees, unhedged JPY. Time works *against* a console cycle; time works *for* an 8.4% FCF yield.
  **Note the elegant offset:** Nintendo's largest cost headwind is memory pricing, driven by the same AI capex the operator's book is long. Worth revisiting on the **November 2026 Q2 print**, which is its own binary.
- **Rheinmetall (RHM.DE)** — −43% from high on a **2% guidance cut**, backlog €80.4bn (5.7 years, 70% firm). But H1 free cash flow **−€1.6bn** on a €6.2bn inventory build, four consensus misses in six quarters, and a monopsony customer that just cancelled a €12.8bn frigate programme after spending €2.3bn. The bear case here has *data*, not just narrative. Its own analyst put it at ~50/50 vs NVDA.
- **argenx (ARGX)** — +60% product sales, 90% gross margin, +146% operating profit, lead competitor's asset discontinued. Excellent. Rejected as an *addition* because it is **9% off its all-time high**: it is a fifth bet of the same shape as the existing four — high-quality growth at a full multiple, failing the same way if growth decelerates. Intuit is the only candidate whose multiple has *already* compressed.

## I7 — The joint-state observation that actually drove the choice

🟡 **My model, and it needs an honest caveat attached.**

Every one of the four holdings — NVDA, PLTR, AMZN, LLY — is the same *shape* of bet: a high-quality grower at a full multiple. Each fails the same way: growth decelerates, multiple compresses. That is one risk expressed four times.

**The caveat, computed rather than assumed:** the four names' average *daily* pairwise correlation is only **+0.14**. So the "three-quarters of the book is one trade" framing is not supported by realised co-movement. The reconciliation I believe is right: **daily correlation measures noise co-movement; shared failure mode is a regime-level property.** Over a 6–24 month horizon it is the failure mode that matters, not the daily beta. But I want the operator to see that I checked, and that the check partially cut against me.

Intuit is the only candidate found in the entire sweep whose multiple has already compressed to a level pricing secular decline. Its return does not require growth to accelerate — it requires the market to stop pricing extinction. **That is a different return engine from anything currently in the book**, and it is the actual reason it wins the ranking.
