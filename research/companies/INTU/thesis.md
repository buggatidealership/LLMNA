## TL;DR

The market has taken **57% off Intuit** on a claim — agentic AI commoditises bookkeeping and tax — for which **an adversarial pass found no customer-level evidence whatsoever**: no named competitor with traction, no disclosed customer counts, no accountant-channel defections. Goldman's Sell note names three AI tax entrants and attaches adoption data to none of them, and contains **no QuickBooks model at all** — leaving ~60% of revenue out of the bear case.

But the same adversarial pass found a **different, real problem the sell-side is only half-articulating**: Intuit's growth is **price-carried, not volume-carried**, and the price lever is now being pulled hard enough to be visible.

**The honest thesis is therefore narrower than the one I started with.** It is not "the filings show acceleration and the market missed it" — at segment level that was **wrong**. It is: *the disagreement is almost entirely about the terminal multiple, not about the next two years of earnings.*

## 🔴 Rule #18 — the falsifying case, at full strength, BEFORE the conclusion

Six things an adversarial pass established. Each one damaged the original thesis; they are recorded first because that is the house rule.

1. **"Accelerating" was wrong where it matters.** QuickBooks Online Accounting revenue growth across FY26: **+25% → +24% → +22%**. Monotonic, seasonally neutral, and it is the single cleanest series in the company. My headline framing rested on the 9M consolidated numbers, which are flattered by segment mix.

2. **The growth is all price.** Intuit's own words for FY26 TurboTax: total online units **−2%**, share of e-files **−1 point**, paying units +2%, **ARPU +11%**, pay-nothing customers 8M → 7M. Revenue +7% with units down is not a growth business; it is a harvesting business.

3. **The control experiment ran, and Intuit lost it.** IRS Direct File — TurboTax's free federal competitor — was **killed on 2025-11-07**. FY26 was the first season without it. TurboTax units *still* fell ~2% and e-file share *still* fell ~1 point. The structural threat was removed for free and volume declined anyway.

4. **The price lever is visibly straining.** QuickBooks Online list prices rose again on **2026-08-01** — Essentials +42%, Plus +41%, **Advanced +70%** — on top of a separate May-2026 increase. Simultaneously Intuit launched **free / Lite / Ledger** tiers at the bottom. Taking 40–70% at the top while giving the base away is what a company does when it can no longer price the low end. And QBO growth *decelerated* through it.

5. **My best margin argument was built on a framing management explicitly rejects.** I had modelled $479–799M/yr of AI-driven flow-through from the 17% headcount cut. CEO Goodarzi, Q3 call, verbatim: **"This was not about AI."** His stated reasons: management layers, "coordination heavy roles," duplication, and *"sizing and resizing Mailchimp."* **That arithmetic is withdrawn.**

6. **The marginal bear sits BELOW today's price.** Goldman PT **$276** (Sell, 2026-06-02), Stifel **$275**. This is not a de-rating that has overshot into capitulation with everyone already out — the incremental seller sees another ~15% down. Also: **no insider open-market buying** with the stock down 58%.

**Plus two quality flags.** **Mailchimp** revenue is *declining* year-on-year and is being "rightsized," yet goodwill stands at **$13,982M** against cumulative impairment of only **$114M** — goodwill is **15.7% of market cap**. And **33.6% of the non-GAAP EPS number is add-backs** (SBC alone 9.1% of revenue), which is why this file leads on GAAP.

**If you stop reading here, the fair summary is: this is a decelerating, price-carried franchise with a goodwill overhang, and two brokers have targets below the current price.** Everything below is the argument that this is nevertheless mispriced.

## The thesis that survives

### 1. The fear the market is actually pricing has no evidence behind it

An adversarial sweep specifically hunting for displacement evidence — named companies, funding rounds with traction, disclosed customer counts, accountant-channel commentary, trade-press coverage through 2026-08-07 — **found none.** Accounting Today's technology section carries no QuickBooks-displacement story; its 2026-07-27 headline runs the other way (*"Clients value human relationships over AI efficiencies"*). No analyst on the Q3 call named a single competitor.

⚠️ **Honest limit:** that agent's search budget was exhausted mid-task and Reddit/fallback engines were blocked. **Xero's FY26 North America subscriber disclosure is the single highest-value unchecked datapoint** and is explicitly flagged as an open hole, not a closed question. Absence of evidence here is partly absence of search.

### 2. The mix is moving toward the least substitutable revenue, not away from it

| Line | Growth | Why it resists an LLM |
|---|---|---|
| **TurboTax Live** | **+36% to $2.8B**, now **>50% of TurboTax revenue**; customers +38%, retention +2pts | It is a *human expert* service. Goldman's "$0.12 per return vs $162" comparison is against DIY software and does not touch it |
| **QBO Advanced + Intuit Enterprise Suite** | **~+38%**; IES contracts **+37% sequentially** | Mid-market, multi-entity, direct-sales-led, implementation-locked |
| **Online Services** (money + payroll) | +15%, of which **money +$107M, payroll +$55M** | Regulated money transmission and payroll tax liability. An agent does not become a licensed money transmitter in 50 states |

Goodarzi's rebuttal is the correct one and is not addressed anywhere in the bear literature: *"the 88% of our TAM that go to somebody else to do their taxes for them has nothing to do with how good software gets. This is all about confidence."*

### 3. The disagreement is about the multiple, and the bear's own model concedes the earnings

This is the load-bearing observation of the whole file.

**Goldman is the Sell case.** Goldman models FY28 non-GAAP EPS of **$28.55** — i.e. from FY26's $23.83, **+9.5%/yr compounding even in the bear case**. Their $276 target implies **9.7× their own FY28 number**. Today's $325.25 implies **11.4× the bear's own FY28 EPS.**

**You are not betting the bear's earnings model is wrong. You are betting ~10× is too low a terminal multiple** for a business that is ~90% recurring, runs a **31.6% GAAP operating margin**, converts to an **~8.4% FCF yield**, returns **~4.9% of market cap a year in buybacks**, and has held its diluted share count **flat for four years** (and shrinking now: 276M vs 282M, −2.1%).

Reverse-DCF (10% discount, 10-year fade — `facts.md` §5): at $325.25 the market is pricing **−2.5% cash-flow growth in perpetuity**. That is a verdict on Intuit's existence, not on its next two years.

### 4. Where the deceleration actually sits

| Q3 FY26 | Growth |
|---|---|
| Consumer / TurboTax | **+8%** ← the part that is slowing |
| Global Business Solutions | **+15%** (FY guide **raised** to ~16%) ← the part the AI story is about |
| QuickBooks Online Accounting | **+22%** |

Consolidated FY26 growth decelerates +18% → +17% → +10% → ~+11% implied. **But the slowing quarter is the tax quarter, in a season when industry-wide IRS filings fell ~30bp** — and the segment the market says AI will destroy is the one growing fastest and the one whose guidance was raised. That disaggregation is the case in one table. It does not refute point 1 of the bear list — QBO *is* decelerating — but it locates the decay.

## Falsifiers — rebuilt on metrics Intuit actually discloses

The adversarial pass killed my first falsifier set: I had written one against QuickBooks Online customer count, and **Intuit has declined to disclose that number for four straight quarters.** A falsifier against an undisclosed metric is decoration. Rebuilt, each with its **BLIND-CHECK** line (Principle #51):

**F1 — QBO Accounting revenue growth prints below 18% in any quarter.**
Series is 25 → 24 → 22. Sub-18 says the price lever has stopped covering volume decay.
*BLIND-CHECK:* distinguishes price-exhaustion from tax-season phasing · reads on a seasonally-neutral disclosed line · **goes blind if Intuit stops breaking out QBO Accounting.** It re-segmented once already (2025-08-01, Consumer + Credit Karma + ProTax merged). **If this line disappears at the Sep-17 Investor Day, treat the disappearance itself as F1 firing.**

**F2 — FY27 TurboTax online units decline >2% again while ARPU growth falls below ~8%.**
Price running out with volume still leaking. FY26 was −2% units / +11% ARPU.
*BLIND-CHECK:* distinguishes a pricing franchise from a shrinking one · reads on the units/ARPU decomposition Intuit publishes in the Q4 release · **goes blind if that decomposition is withdrawn — withdrawal fires the falsifier.**

**F3 — FY27 revenue guidance below ~10%, or FY27 non-GAAP EPS growth below ~10%, at the Aug-25 print.**
The bear's own model has +9.5%/yr. Guiding below that concedes the bear case from the company's own mouth.
*BLIND-CHECK:* distinguishes decay from cliff · reads on the first FY27 guide · goes blind only if guidance is withheld, which would itself be the signal.

**F4 — Mailchimp goodwill impairment above ~$2B.**
Goodwill $13,982M vs $114M cumulative impairment on a declining asset. A large write-down validates that the conglomerate is worth less than the sum claimed.
*BLIND-CHECK:* reads on a balance-sheet line at any quarterly print; cannot go blind.
