# LLY (Eli Lilly) — Interpretations

My inferred reads. Kept separate from `facts.md`. Dated 2026-08-08, first substantive pass — adversarial test of the operator's thesis: *"consumers don't want a needle, they'd rather swallow a pill, Lilly understands this and wins the oral weight-loss market."* Two pre-registered doubts under test: (1) efficacy trade-off, (2) capture-not-invention. All 🟢/🟡/🔴 truth-tier tags per convention.

## DOUBT 1 — the efficacy trade-off is real, and it is the crux

🟢 Cross-trial (NOT head-to-head — no RCT compares orforglipron to tirzepatide or semaglutide directly; flagged explicitly in `facts.md`), the oral options lose meaningfully to the injectables on the primary weight-loss endpoint:

| Drug | Route | Trial | Duration | Top dose Δwt | Discontinuation (AE/GI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orforglipron | Oral | ATTAIN-1 | 72wk | 12.4% | 10.3% (AE, 36mg) vs 2.6% placebo |
| Orforglipron | Oral | ATTAIN-2 (+T2D) | 72wk | 10.5% | not itemized in this pass |
| Oral semaglutide | Oral | OASIS 4 | 64wk | 16.6% (adherent) | not itemized in this pass |
| Tirzepatide | Injectable | SURMOUNT-1 | 72wk | 22.5% | 4.3% (GI, 15mg) vs 0.5% placebo |
| Semaglutide | Injectable | STEP 1 | 68wk | 14.9% | not itemized in this pass |

🟡 The gap is largest between orforglipron and tirzepatide (12.4% vs 22.5%, roughly half — this matches the "tirzepatide ~2x orforglipron" framing multiple outlets converged on independently, e.g. Ro/USNews/Maven/GoodRx — directional cross-trial convergence, not head-to-head proof). The gap is much smaller between oral and injectable *semaglutide specifically* (16.6% vs 14.9% — oral actually reads slightly higher in this cross-trial comparison, though OASIS 4's 16.6% is an "adherent-only" completer analysis, which likely inflates it relative to STEP 1's intent-to-treat-style reporting — **this is a like-for-unlike comparison and the true gap is probably smaller than it looks, possibly favoring the injectable once analysis methods are matched**).

🟡 **The discontinuation data cuts against the "convenience wins" framing in a way I did not expect going in.** Orforglipron's AE-driven discontinuation (5.1–10.3% across doses) is higher in absolute terms than tirzepatide's GI-specific discontinuation (1.9–4.3%) — even though these aren't a clean apples-to-apples pair (one is "all adverse events," the other is "GI events specifically," so orforglipron's true GI-only discontinuation is probably lower than its all-AE figure). Even with that caveat, there is no evidence in what I could find that the oral small molecule is *better tolerated* than the injectable — if anything the opposite. **This directly weakens the "no needle, so people stick with it" logic**: oral small-molecule GLP-1s hit the gut wall of the GI tract directly and continuously (daily dosing) rather than via a weekly depot, and nothing in the trial data supports an assumption that pills are gentler.

🔴 **Adjudication on Doubt 1: PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, with a real split inside "oral."** Small-molecule oral (orforglipron) trails injectable materially on efficacy (~45% smaller top-dose effect vs tirzepatide) with no compensating tolerability edge — the trade-off the operator worried about is real for Lilly's specific oral asset. But oral peptide (Novo's oral semaglutide, OASIS 4) reads much closer to its own injectable sibling — the "oral is a downgrade" framing may be more of an orforglipron-specific finding than a category-wide one. **This matters for the Lilly thesis specifically**: Lilly's flagship oral bet (orforglipron) is the small-molecule with the bigger efficacy gap, not the peptide with the smaller one. If patients/doctors are efficacy-maximizing rather than convenience-maximizing, Lilly's own injectable (Zepbound) remains the stronger clinical case than Lilly's own oral (Foundayo) — Lilly is in the position of building the pill that competes with itself on a weaker efficacy footing.

## DOUBT 2 — capture, not invention

🟢 Novo was first to market with an oral obesity GLP-1 (oral Wegovy, approved 2025-12-22) — roughly 3 months ahead of Lilly's Foundayo (approved 2026-04-01). 🟢 Launch-trajectory data (IQVIA, week-13 script counts: oral Wegovy >105,000 vs Foundayo 19,550) shows Novo's oral pill outpacing Lilly's oral pill on uptake so far, the inverse of the injectable market where Zepbound (Lilly) leads Wegovy injectable (Novo) with ~60% share.

🟡 **Reading this together: "Lilly wins because Lilly understands the pill preference" does not fit the launch data.** Novo got to the oral form factor first and is winning early oral share; Lilly's edge in the category has historically been injectable efficacy (Zepbound's dual GIP/GLP-1 action), which is precisely the edge orforglipron does NOT carry over into pill form (single-action GLP-1 only, smaller effect size per Doubt 1). The operator's thesis conflates "Lilly is a strong GLP-1 company" (🟢 true, on the injectable side) with "Lilly wins the *oral* market specifically" (🔴 not supported by the launch data available as of 2026-08-08 — Novo is currently ahead in the pill category it invented).

🟡 Patent runway favors nobody being dislodged soon: semaglutide protected to ~2031 core / possibly ~2041 via later patents; tirzepatide to 2036 core / 2039 formulation; orforglipron's core patent (filed 2018) points to expiry "late 2030s" (not independently pinned to an exact date in this pass — flagged as a gap). **No generic/biosimilar entry risk exists inside a normal 6–24 month investing horizon for any of the three assets.** This removes one entire vector of "capture" risk (patent cliff) but says nothing about competitive capture from OTHER branded entrants.

🟡 Competitive field is wider than "Novo vs Lilly": Structure Therapeutics' aleniglipron is Phase 3-bound (Q3 2026 start) with Phase 2 efficacy (11.3–15.3% placebo-adjusted) roughly comparable to orforglipron's — a third oral GLP-1 could be on the market within the investing horizon window depending on Phase 3 timelines (typically 2+ years, so likely arriving toward the back half of a 24-month horizon at the earliest). Novo's amycretin (oral + injectable, GLP-1/amylin dual) is a longer-dated threat (GlobalData estimates 2030 US approval) but is Novo's *next-generation* answer, not a stand-still. **Lilly does not have the oral category to itself, and its head start over Structure Therapeutics is not obviously durable given orforglipron's efficacy sits in a similar band to Structure's Phase 2 data.**

🔴 **Adjudication on Doubt 2: CONFIRMED as a live risk, arguably already visible in the data.** Being a strong company in GLP-1s generally (🟢, true, injectable-led) is not the same as capturing the oral sub-market specifically (🔴, not supported — Novo currently leads there). The "Lilly wins oral because Lilly understands the pill preference" framing has no evidentiary support distinguishing it from "Novo got there first and executed faster," which is the more parsimonious read of the launch data.

## Pricing / payer — second-order read

🟡 List prices for orals are NOT meaningfully discounted vs. injectables (oral Wegovy list $1,349.02/mo = identical to injectable Wegovy's list). The pricing signal that oral is "cheaper" comes entirely from **self-pay cash-price tiers** ($149–$299/mo oral vs $299+/mo injectable), which is a Lilly/Novo commercial-strategy choice (LillyDirect / NovoCare bypass-insurance channel), not a structural cost advantage of pill manufacturing over injectable pens. 🔴 **This means the "higher volume, lower profit per unit" risk the operator's brief flags is real at the self-pay/cash-pay channel specifically, but does NOT show up at list price** — so the margin risk is conditional on how much volume moves through the discounted self-pay channel vs. full-price insured channel. I could not find channel-mix data (% of scripts self-pay vs. insured) to resolve this either way — **flagged as NOT ESTABLISHED**.

🟡 Payer behavior is genuinely under-documented in open sources at the granularity that would matter (payer-by-payer tiering rules, PA rates by formulation). The directional signal (some plans tier oral vs injectable differently) is real but not itemized enough to say which way the incentive nets out. This is a case where **"payers decide," per the operator's own framing, is correctly emphasized but I could not verify the mechanism in enough detail — NOT ESTABLISHED beyond the general PA/step-therapy backdrop.**

## Real-world preference — the needle-aversion claim has actual survey support, but it's stated preference not revealed preference

🟡 The Currax survey (71% prefer pill; n>1,000) is the only preference data point found, and it is stated preference (asked directly), not revealed preference (actual choice behavior when both options are covered and priced identically). 🟢 The closest thing to revealed preference — actual launch uptake — shows oral capturing only ~20% of the US branded obesity market against ~80% for injectables as of Q2 2026, months after both oral options were available. **That gap between "71% say they'd prefer a pill" and "~20% market share for pills" is the single most important tension in this whole brief.** Plausible explanations, none confirmed: efficacy-aware patients/doctors self-select into the stronger injectable; weekly (not daily) injection burden is genuinely smaller than survey respondents imagine when asked in the abstract (consistent with the operator's own pre-registered caution that "a weekly injection is not a daily injection"); payer/PA friction is asymmetric; or the oral rollout is simply too recent (3–7 months) for revealed behavior to catch up to stated preference. **I could not determine which of these dominates — flagged as an open question, not resolved in this pass.**

## Second-order names — worth tracking, not yet built into theses

🟡 Casualties (structural, if oral scales further): bariatric-surgery-adjacent device makers (gastric band/balloon market already shrinking, ~$49M→$34M by 2035 per one estimate); GLP-1 pen/autoinjector suppliers (Ypsomed, SHL Medical, Phillips-Medisize) IF injectable share genuinely erodes — but current data (80% injectable share) says this is not yet happening at scale. Restaurant/CPG names already show measurable revenue drag ($32–44B annual US CPG estimate) — this is a GLP-1-class effect (route-agnostic), not oral-specific.
🟡 Beneficiaries: telehealth distribution platforms pivoting to branded (Hims & Hers post-Novo partnership) sit downstream of BOTH oral and injectable branded volume — not a pure oral-scaling play. Compounding pharmacies are now a shrinking channel post-shortage-resolution (legal only for patient-specific exceptions) — **compounding is not a live grey-market threat to Lilly/Novo pricing power the way it was in 2023–2024**, which is a point IN FAVOR of the operator's thesis holding for the branded originators generally (though it does not adjudicate oral vs. injectable specifically).
🔴 None of these second-order names are held or thesis-built in this pass — flagged for a future DEEP-DIG if the operator wants to size any of them.
