## §3 — N-TH ORDER TRACE: the Apple/component-cost item (Tier-2 but structurally the most interesting)

**Trigger:** Apple guides to slower growth explicitly citing **higher component costs**, having beaten on every line.

**1st order (P>80%)** — Memory/component scarcity is now a *disclosed* margin and revenue constraint at the highest-quality hardware buyer in the world. Not an inference from suppliers; a buyer-side admission.

**2nd order (P~60%)** — **Knock-on to the non-integrated OEM cohort.** Apple has the best supply agreements on earth and still could not remedy it. **Named downstream casualties: Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Transsion, and the PC OEMs** — they pay the same input inflation without Apple's contract terms *or* Samsung's captive fab. Samsung's own MX division already printed a first-ever quarterly loss from its own DS division's prices, which is the intra-company proof of the same mechanism.

**3rd order (P~40%)** — **Bypass routes and who wins them.** Substitution/down-spec is failing (Apple tried); second-source (CXMT/YMTC) is ramping but not qualified at premium tiers; **demand destruction at the consumer end is the bypass that is actually firing.** Ripple: pricing power stays with the memory *recipients* longer than consensus assumes, and the casualty list is the handset/PC assemblers rather than the memory buyers' suppliers.

**4th order (P~20%)** — If component inflation persists into 2027 (Samsung guides the shortage to intensify then persist through 2028), the durable consequence is a **margin transfer from device assemblers to memory makers** large enough to show up in national CPI baskets — which is the same channel Warsh reportedly named at the FOMC, and therefore the mechanism that would make **H1 and H3 the same hypothesis**. That is the open re-spec, and it is why item #4 in the routing table is the highest-value fire of the morning.
