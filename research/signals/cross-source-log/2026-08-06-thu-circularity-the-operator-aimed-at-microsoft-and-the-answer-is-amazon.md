**The famous ~70% divides a T1 numerator by an extrapolated denominator** — a non-GAAP *annualized run-rate* from the March-2026 quarter, grown forward at 123%, for a metric **Microsoft has disclosed exactly twice in its history and DID NOT UPDATE at Q4 FY26.** Microsoft has **no AI revenue line item, no Azure AI line item, no AI segment.** The 7.3% and 11.3% are the only filing-grade shares.

🔴 **The circularity claim itself is REFUTED as framed.** Microsoft has funded **$11.9B cumulative since 2019** against **$24.1B of revenue in FY2026 alone**. Capital supplied is ~49% of a *single year's* revenue and **4.8% of the $250B commitment.** It cannot be its own money round-tripping.

**The defensible Microsoft flags are different ones:** (a) **$6.0B receivable** on one counterparty ≈ **91 days** — a credit/collection question; (b) **7.3% of total company revenue** from a single customer; (c) the **composition is undisclosed** — Azure consumption vs revenue-share vs other is a single blended number, and rev-share is a *cost to OpenAI on Microsoft-distributed products*, not Azure demand.

**Stale-figure flags:** the 10-K says **~25%** as-converted; the Oct-2025 announcement said **27%** — likely dilution from OpenAI's ~$110–122B round at $852B post-money, unreconciled by Microsoft. **Citing 27% today is stale.** And **Nvidia→OpenAI settled at ~$30B, NOT $100B** — Huang: *"probably not in the cards."* **Any 2026 citation of "$100B" is recycling a Sept-2025 LOI.**

### 🔴 §4b — MICROSOFT'S "$15B CAPEX CUT" IS NOT A CUT. It is a lease reclassification.

CY2026 guidance moved **$190B → ~$175B**. CFO, verbatim: *"effective at the start of FY27, we are extending the estimated useful life of our data centers and office buildings from 15 to 25 years… This change affects only the timing of future depreciation,"* and separately *"outside of this useful-life impact, our calendar year 2026 capex investment expectations remain unchanged."*

**Mechanism:** extending building economic life changes the ASC 842 lease-classification test (lease term as % of economic life), pushing datacenter leases from **finance** (which sits in the capex line) to **operating** (which does not). **One accounting-estimate change simultaneously lowers future depreciation, moves ~$15B of real spend off the headline capex line, and converts on-balance-sheet finance-lease liabilities into operating leases. Nothing real changed.**

## §7 — WHAT THIS CHANGES

**N2 (capex→backlog) is upgraded from "the instrument I can build" to "the instrument that already produced its most important reading."** The 48% concentration is the finding; the ratio was only the route to it.

**New candidate check, generalising from Amazon:** *what share of a vendor's backlog is owed by counterparties that vendor capitalised?* **Computable wherever both legs are disclosed — which today is Amazon only.** For everyone else the answer is *not disclosed*, and **that absence is now a scored attribute rather than a blank.**

**Falsifying case (Rule #18), argued at strength:** a large funded-counterparty share is **not automatically fragility.** Amazon's stakes marked **+$62.8B in H1** — the counterparties got *more* valuable, and a supplier taking equity in a customer whose value compounds has captured upside a pure vendor would have missed. **The fragility is conditional on the counterparties' ability to pay from external revenue, which I did not verify and which no filing establishes.** The honest statement is that Amazon has converted vendor risk into equity risk and disclosed neither leg as such — not that it is impaired.
